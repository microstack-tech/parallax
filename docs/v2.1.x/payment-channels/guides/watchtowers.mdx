---
title: "Watchtowers"
---

A watchtower is an always-on service that watches the blockchain for cheating close attempts against your channels and challenges them on your behalf while you're offline. This guide covers both sides: delegating to a tower as a wallet user, and running one.

Before either, one sentence of trust model, because it shapes everything: **a tower can be lazy, but never a thief.** The material you hand it fits exactly one on-chain action — submitting your *true latest state* as a challenge. It cannot close your channels, cannot spend anything, cannot lie on your behalf (the contract only accepts states both channel parties signed, newer than what's being challenged). A malicious tower's ceiling is doing nothing, plus knowing your channel balances. That is why delegating to several towers costs nothing and conflicts with nothing.

## Using a tower (wallet side)

Add the tower's npub to your channel config:

```toml
[channels.towers]
npubs = ["8f3a…tower-npub…c2d1"]
```

That's all. From then on your wallet automatically sends every newly countersigned state to each listed tower over the relays (encrypted end-to-end — relays never see the contents), retransmitting until that tower acknowledges receipt — each tower's delivery is tracked separately, and acknowledgements are only accepted from the towers you configured. On startup it also brings towers up to date on every open channel, including states completed offline over QR; this catch-up runs whether or not relay self-backup is enabled.

When do you want one?

| Your situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Phone wallet, online most days, default ~24h challenge window | Optional but cheap peace of mind |
| Going off-grid longer than your challenge window | Yes — this is the exact gap towers close |
| Merchant with an always-on daemon | Your daemon already self-watches; a tower adds redundancy |
| High-value channel with a long challenge window | Yes, ideally two independent towers |

The tower earns a small fixed refund (0.01 LAX, paid out of the cheater's penalty) each time it successfully challenges — enough to cover its gas, by design not enough to make "challenging" a business that would attract the wrong incentives.

## Running a tower

A tower is the same `parallax-wallet` binary in a different mode. It needs a keyfile holding a little LAX for challenge gas, a synced node, and a config:

```toml
[node]
rpc = "ws://127.0.0.1:8546"
confirmations = 3

[registries]
  [[registries.v1]]
  address = "0x…"
  chain_id = 2110

[nostr]
relays = ["wss://relay.damus.io", "wss://your-relay.example"]

[tower]
enabled = true
delegators = [                      # npubs allowed to delegate to you
  "31e1…alice…9df",
  "23cc…bob…61d",
]
open_registration = false           # true = accept anyone (see below)
min_discrepancy_wei = "1000000000000000"   # ignore closes off by < 0.001 LAX
max_delegations_per_npub = 1000
```

```bash
parallax-wallet tower --config tower.toml --channeldata /var/lib/parallax-tower \
  --passwordfile pw.txt tower-key.json
```

```
tower running: address 0x9A2c…11E npub 8f3a…c2d1 (2 delegators)
```

What it does, continuously:

1. **Accepts delegations** (encrypted states from your delegators), verifying each against the chain — the signatures must belong to that channel's actual on-chain participants — and keeping only the newest per channel.
2. **Watches every channel close** on the registry contract, not just delegated ones — a single event stream, cheap to follow.
3. **Challenges stale closes**: when a close's state is older than a delegated one, it submits the delegated state. The submission is managed until mined — fees are bumped automatically if the transaction stalls, and a loud alarm fires if the challenge window is running out — because landing this one transaction is the entire job.
4. **Collects the refund** from the cheater's penalty on settlement.

Operational knobs:

- `min_discrepancy_wei` is tower economics, not user safety: closes whose error is smaller than this aren't worth your gas. Users' own wallets still challenge any discrepancy — it's their money.
- `open_registration = true` turns the tower public: anyone may delegate, bounded by `max_delegations_per_npub` (storage is the only thing a stranger can consume — a delegation is a few kilobytes, and a bogus one is rejected against the chain at intake).
- Fund the tower key modestly and monitor it; each challenge costs gas up front, recouped by the refund.

## What a tower learns

Delegated states reveal the channel's participants and balance history to the tower — a privacy cost, not a security one. If that matters for your use case, run your own tower; it is a single process and a systemd unit away.
