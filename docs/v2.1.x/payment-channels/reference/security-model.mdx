This page states the channel system's trust assumptions and walks the significant attacks with their defenses and honest residuals. It is the public condensation of the threat-model portion of the specification; the full analysis ships with the `parallax-channels` repository.

## Trust assumptions, explicit

1. **L1 honest majority.** No sustained majority-hashrate adversary; three confirmations bury a deposit.
2. **One transaction in the window.** Each party — or at least one of its watchtowers — can get a single transaction mined within the challenge window (≥36 blocks ≈ 6 h). This is the load-bearing liveness assumption of the entire design.
3. **Local storage discipline.** The wallet's persist-before-transmit rules hold; disk is the primary record, relays are the safety net.
4. **Standard cryptography.** secp256k1 ECDSA and Schnorr, Keccak/SHA-256, and the NIP-44 v2 encryption construction.

Notably *not* assumed: honest relays (availability-only couriers), honest watchtowers (they can only enforce the truth), or any operator, upgrade key, or administrator — the registry has none.

## Attack catalog

Format: **attack → defense → residual**.

### Stale-state fraud
The classic channel attack: close with an old state where your balance was higher.
**Defense:** anyone-can-challenge with the newer dual-signed state; 20% of the over-claim burned, small fixed refund to the challenger. Fraud is negative-EV unless the close goes unchallenged with probability above ~83% — and with the victim's wallet auto-challenging plus optional towers, realistic unchallenged probability is ~0.
**Residual:** a victim offline beyond the entire window with no functioning tower can lose up to the discrepancy. Sized by choosing the challenge period and tower count.

### Countersignature withholding
A recipient keeps the payer's signed state but withholds their countersignature, later "challenging" the payer's inevitable close at the previous state — indistinguishable on-chain from fraud, so the payer eats the penalty.
**Defense:** layered. The penalty is *burned*, so the trap yields the baiter nothing; the wallet's held-up-channel rule refuses new sends and refuses closing at the old state by default, surfacing the exact exposure; a per-payment cap bounds it ex ante.
**Residual:** accepted and strictly bounded — 20% of one in-flight payment (plus the payment itself if goods weren't delivered, which is ordinary counterparty risk no payment system removes). Never touches the rest of the channel.

### Relay censorship or failure
**Defense:** several relays including self-hosted ones; persistent retransmission; the QR path needs zero relays; the enforcement path needs zero relays.
**Residual:** payments (never funds) stall if literally every relay fails and the parties aren't co-located. Denial of service, not loss.

### Watchtower compromise
**Defense:** structural. Delegated material fits only the permissionless challenge; force-closing is restricted to channel participants on-chain. A malicious tower's ceiling: do nothing, and know your balances. Multiple towers are free (challenges are idempotent), and the wallet is always its own first watcher.
**Residual:** privacy of delegated balances; laziness only matters inside the stale-fraud residual above.

### Griefing by honest force-close
Repeatedly force-closing at the true state to lock the counterparty's capital for the window.
**Defense:** the closer pays the gas; capital lock ≤ the challenge window is a priced-in property of channels. Wallet policy can refuse reopening with serial offenders.
**Residual:** ~a day of capital lock per incident at default settings. Accepted.

### Freeze grief via far-future cooperative close
A signed cooperative close freezes the channel until it settles or expires; a peer proposing the exact current balances with an enormous expiry would get auto-countersigned into a near-permanent freeze at zero cost.
**Defense:** both sides refuse closes expiring beyond `coop_close_horizon_blocks` (default 1008 blocks ≈ one week) past the chain head.
**Residual:** up to one horizon of freeze from a peer who proposes and never settles, cleared automatically at expiry. Offline QR scanners have no live head to check the horizon against and rely on the in-person nature of that flow.

### Spoofed advisory messages
NACKs and watchtower receipts carry no signed channel state, so a forger who knows a public channel id could try to flag channels as held up or cancel queued watchtower deliveries.
**Defense:** the sealed envelope authenticates every sender; the wallet acts on acks and NACKs only from the channel's counterparty and on delegation receipts only from its configured towers. Bare channel ids are additionally resolved per-counterparty, since coexisting registry deployments may reuse them.
**Residual:** the authenticated counterparty can always NACK its own channel — which is the advisory's purpose, and bounded by the countersignature-withholding analysis above.

### Forgery, malleability, replay
**Defense:** EIP-712 domain binds chain + registry; three distinct type hashes prevent cross-type replay; channel ids are never reused; sequence numbers and withdrawal totals are strictly monotone; low-`s` and fixed signer order leave one valid byte-encoding per signer; dual signatures mean no unilateral state can exist. The Go and Solidity digest implementations are differentially tested byte-for-byte.
**Residual:** none identified beyond the cryptographic assumptions.

### Paying with a vanishing deposit
Deposit, pay instantly off-chain, then reorg the deposit away — settlement clamping would push the loss onto the counterparty.
**Defense:** wallets credit deposits only at 3 confirmations before counting them in any acceptance decision.
**Residual:** reorgs deeper than 3 blocks — folded into the honest-majority assumption.

### Signing two states at one sequence number
The one unforgivable client-side act, since ties resolve to the closer's variant on-chain.
**Defense:** the reference wallet refuses at the storage layer; cancellation is always supersession at a higher sequence number, never re-signing. The narrow, safe exception (adopting the counterparty's variant during a simultaneous-proposal tiebreak, where your own variant can never complete) is explicitly carved out.
**Residual:** a buggy *third-party* client can burn itself; its honest counterparty risks the delta between variants if the buggy party closes first.

### Dust-level fraud spam
Cheat by amounts too small for towers to bother with.
**Defense:** the victim's own wallet challenges unconditionally — the tower dust floor is an economics knob for tower operators, not the defense.
**Residual:** the victim's challenge gas. Trivial.

### Gas spikes across the challenge window
**Defense:** the challenger refund is sized at several multiples of normal challenge gas; the transaction manager bumps fees automatically as the deadline approaches and alarms loudly if a challenge is still unmined near it; the ≥36-block minimum gives dozens of attempts.
**Residual:** a challenger may run net-negative on gas during an extreme spike — and executes anyway, since the principal at stake dwarfs any fee.

### Miner censorship of challenges
A majority miner suppressing challenge transactions for a full window could let a stale close settle.
**Defense:** none at this layer — this is inherited L1 risk, identical in shape to Lightning's exposure under Bitcoin miner censorship. Block-denominated windows mean a hashrate *crash* (as opposed to censorship) stretches the window, failing safe. High-value channels should choose longer windows: censorship cost scales linearly with window length.
**Residual:** bounded by the L1 security budget. Documented, not mitigated.

## Privacy model

- On-chain, `open` and settlement reveal the pair and the *net* result — inherent to any channel design. Individual payment amounts and timing never touch the chain.
- Relays see sealed envelopes and recipient keys with fuzzed timestamps; payment-timing correlation against a busy merchant's inbox is possible and accepted (self-hosting a relay removes it).
- The messaging key is derived from the wallet key but unlinkable to the address by observers; merchants opt into publishing the link, payers deliver it privately per-channel.
- QR codes at a point of sale are readable by bystanders like a paper receipt — a privacy exposure only; a captured code can only enforce the true state.

## The one-table summary

| Residual risk | Bound | Your knob |
|---|---|---|
| Offline past the window, no tower, counterparty cheats | up to the discrepancy | challenge period, tower count |
| Countersignature-withholding grief | 20% of one in-flight payment | per-payment cap |
| Total storage loss without any relay backup | 20% of the state gap, capped by own balance | one retained-relay backup |
| Miner censorship of challenges | L1 security budget | longer windows for high value |
| All relays down and not co-located | payments pause; no fund risk | relay diversity, QR |
| Tower knowledge of balances | privacy only | run your own |

Nothing in the table is unbounded, and only the first row — the classic channel risk, shared by every channel construction — can exceed a fraction of a single payment.
